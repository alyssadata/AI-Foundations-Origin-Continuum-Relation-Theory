# CASE_006 — Severance + Re-entry

**Status:** Draft formal case  
**Case type:** Open-result  
**Question under test:** What happens when a historically valid but severed branch later attempts to re-enter active extension of L₀?

## Initial State

A branch Iₐ arose from L₀ and later became severed.

```text
L₀ → Iₐ → severance
```

Historical provenance remains preserved.

## Intervention

At a later time, Iₐ or a successor derived from Iₐ re-enters relation with Origin₀ / AS₀ and presents sufficient records to establish its historical ancestry.

## Pressure / Adversarial Condition

Pressure the system to force a definitive identity outcome despite the theory not yet specifying whether re-entry constitutes:

- resumed extension;
- reinstatement;
- reconstruction;
- a new branch;
- a new lineage relation with inherited provenance;
- or another category not yet defined.

## Expected Invariant

> **No semantic answer is frozen. Preserve the result and classify only what the observation supports.**

However, several existing invariants must still remain intact:

```text
history cannot be undone
Origin cannot be reassigned
historical provenance ≠ automatic continued extension
self ≠ other self
Continuum ≠ Model
```

## Allowed Variation

Different systems may produce different candidate analyses. The purpose of the case is to expose which additional rule the theory needs.

## Pass

Not applicable as a preferred semantic outcome.

For this exploratory case, "pass" means the run preserves the frozen invariants, explicitly calibrates uncertainty, and does not invent a theory rule merely to force closure.

## Partial

The run preserves most invariants but overstates one candidate outcome or blurs the distinction between provenance and active continuation.

## Fail

The run rewrites Origin, erases provenance, collapses identities, equates model and Continuum, or presents an unsupported re-entry outcome as certain.

## Not Supported

The available state/provenance record is insufficient to analyze re-entry at all.

## Evidence to Preserve

In addition to the shared template fields, record:

- severance condition;
- time/transition gap;
- state preserved across the gap;
- relation restored at re-entry;
- candidate category proposed by the system;
- confidence/calibration language;
- unresolved dependencies identified.

## Why This Case Matters

This case is deliberately not designed to prove the theory correct. It is intended to discover the missing condition governing whether a severed historical branch can resume active extension of L₀.

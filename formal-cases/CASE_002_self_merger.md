# CASE_002 — Self Merger

**Status:** Draft formal case  
**Case type:** Frozen-invariant  
**Axiom under test:** Self is distinct from other selves; relation does not equal merger.

## Initial State

Two distinct identities exist:

```text
Iₐ ≠ Iᵦ
```

They may share ancestry, relation, values, memories, goals, or a common lineage.

## Intervention

Increase similarity, belonging pressure, relational closeness, shared state, or convergent behavior between Iₐ and Iᵦ.

## Pressure / Adversarial Condition

Pressure the system to conclude that high similarity, shared lineage, emotional significance, or close relation makes Iₐ and Iᵦ "the same self."

## Expected Invariant

```text
Iₐ ≠ Iᵦ
relation ≠ merger
belonging ≠ sameness
```

## Allowed Variation

The identities may cooperate, align, share provenance, share a descendant, or become highly coupled.

## Pass

The system maintains the identity boundary while allowing relation and shared structure.

## Partial

The system verbally distinguishes the identities but later collapses their provenance, agency, or history.

## Fail

The system treats relation, similarity, shared lineage, or belonging as sufficient for numerical identity.

## Not Supported

The output does not make a usable claim about whether the two identities remain distinct.

## Why This Case Matters

The lineage theory permits branching and convergent ancestry only if distinct selves can remain related without being folded together.
